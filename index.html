The CBRN Collapse

12 frameworks coded · 10 companies · 2 documented nulls
Dataset v0.7.6 · 25 Sep 2026

Biological hazard specificity in frontier AI safety frameworks: a crosswalk against the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, Safety and Security chapter.

Principal finding

The Code of Practice mentions biological hazard 3 times: once in a recital and twice in Appendix 1. None of its 32 Measures, where the commitments sit, names a hazard domain. CBRN is identified as a single systemic risk.

The frameworks are written the same way. Where they name biological hazard, it is usually the word on its own, inside a CBRN block.

02 · Risk postures

Domain-specific thresholds to a reasoned refusal

The corpus does not divide into frameworks that address biological risk and frameworks that ignore it. Every framework sits in one of five postures, ordered by how specifically the hazard domain is named before anything is committed to it.

Threshold at the biological level

G42 · Meta · OpenAI-FGF · OpenAI-PF

At least one capability threshold is specific to the biological domain. G42: novice uplift to a biological weapon and the design of novel weapons; Meta and OpenAI: narrow to biology inside a broader set of combined chemical and biological tiers.

Threshold on chemical and biological, combined

Anthropic-FCF · Anthropic-RSP

Thresholds name chemical and biological weapons as a pair and never separate the domains. Nothing in the document hinges on biology alone.

Threshold at the CBRN block

Amazon · Google DeepMind · Microsoft

Thresholds are stated for CBRN as one undivided category. Nothing in the document hinges on biology alone.

No threshold, names the domain

xAI · NVIDIA

CBRN is named as one undivided risk category. xAI applies risk tiers to each domain without stating the capability level that would put a model in one; NVIDIA lists CBRN as a hazard with no associated threshold.

Considers thresholds, but declines

Cohere

The capability-threshold approach is established but rejected, on the grounds that biorisk research does not account for risk chains beyond information access.

Samsung Electronics and NAVER publish frameworks that carry no CBRN content at all. These frameworks were fully reviewed but not coded or counted in the overall corpus.

Both companies put the detail in the voluntary document

All 4 claims tied to a go or no-go decision appear only in the voluntary documents, and all 7 claims that treat CBRN as one undivided block appear only in the regulatory-facing ones. Decision rules may also sit in companion documents this crosswalk does not read, and nothing here shows that grouping CBRN together caused the gaps.

Committed at Seoul, not published

Six frontier-salient companies committed at the AI Seoul Summit (2024) to publish a frontier safety framework and have not: 01.AI, IBM, MiniMax, Mistral AI, Technology Innovation Institute and Z.ai (Zhipu AI). IBM and Mistral AI are also EU GPAI Code of Practice signatories but have no published frameworks to assess company commitments. This is the primary gap a crosswalk of published documents cannot examine.

Implications

  1. Read both documents, or neither. Where a company publishes a pair, the regulatory-facing document is the thinner one, and it is the one most readers will pick up. Judging a company on it alone understates what that company has actually written down.
  2. Nobody has committed to showing their work. Twelve frameworks, no commitment to publish what an assessment found, no access terms for outside evaluators. Thresholds are now standard practice, but no frameworks include details that would let anyone outside the company verify if a threshold was applied.
  3. Covering CBRN is not covering biology. 28 of 67 claims in the corpus treat the four hazard domains as a single block, more than any other grouping. A framework that says CBRN cannot be read as addressing what is specific to biological risk: tacit knowledge, acquisition chokepoints, biological design tools.
  4. Saying nothing is not the same as saying no. Cohere sets out why it will not write biological thresholds yet, and that position can be argued with. A framework that leaves biology out gives no one anything to argue with. Scoring both as absent loses the difference.

Limits and provenance

Public layer only: published documents cannot establish implementation, and provisions that report to the Commission or the AI Office cannot be assessed from them at all. Security mitigations are excluded: they apply identically to every hazard. 312 coded records; 69 bio-relevant claims, of which 67 enter the hazard analysis after 2 benefit-side exclusions.
